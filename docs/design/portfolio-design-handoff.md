**For:** Claude Design · **Epic:** portfolio-analysis (`docs/epics/portfolio-analysis.md`), page feature F-030 ·
**Status:** the page was built in F-030 in the Paper theme (`src/sbretire/web/portfolio/`, viewer.md §4); a later design pass may restyle it. The data contract is now `schemas/portfolio.schema.json` 1.0 · **Date:** 2026-10-18

## 1. What we are designing

A **Portfolio page** that sits beside the existing SBRetire results viewer (same product, same common theme). It
shows the household's investments across five accounts belonging to two people, and answers:

1. **What is it worth today?** One total, split by person and account. The total feeds the retirement model's
   `starting_balance`.
2. **How has it done?** Returns, volatility, drawdowns, income and fees.
3. **What is it invested in?** Sectors, regions, asset classes and currencies, looking through funds.
4. **How efficient is the mix?** Where the current mix sits against a Sharpe-ratio efficient frontier built from
   the current holdings.
5. **What would a more efficient mix look like?** Target weights, the trades to get there, their cost, and what
   changes.

The readers are the same two people: numerate, not professional investors. The tone is calm and explanatory. **This is
analysis, not advice:** the page shows model calculations under stated assumptions and never tells them what to do.

## 4. Page parts and recommended charts

Suggested order: headline → allocation → efficiency → re-weighting → performance → risk detail → income and fees →
holdings table → data and caveats.

1. **Headline.** Total value (large figure), split by person and by account (a stacked bar or small table), and
   the `starting_balance` line with a "copy" button. Key stats as small figures:
   - 5-year annual return, volatility, Sharpe and maximum drawdown;
   - export-period return.
2. **Allocation, now.**
   - *Account → holding* as a **horizontal stacked bar per account** or a **treemap** (area = value).
   - **Asset class**, **region**, **sector** (look-through) and **currency** exposures as **sorted horizontal bars**.
     Bars read better than pies here, and print better in greyscale.
   - A **top look-through exposures** list showing which funds each company comes through, to spot hidden
     concentration.
3. **Efficient frontier** (the centrepiece). A scatter with:
   - volatility on x and annual return on y;
   - the frontier curve;
   - each holding as a labelled dot;
   - the **current** mix, the **minimum-variance** mix and the **maximum-Sharpe** mix as distinct markers;
   - the **capital market line** from the risk-free rate through max-Sharpe;
   - a hover/focus tooltip with each point's weights.

   Say plainly: "built from the last 5 years of monthly returns; past patterns, not a forecast; each holding capped
   at 25%".
4. **Re-weighting** (analysis).
   - A **dumbbell chart**, current weight → target weight per holding.
   - A trades table: action, value, account, estimated cost, total cost.
   - The resulting change in Sharpe and volatility.
   - Mark **cross-account** trades clearly: pension and SIPP money can't move to the ISA or the taxable account,
     so these are illustrative.
   - The wording must stay descriptive ("the model's maximum-Sharpe mix would…"), never "you should…".
5. **Performance.**
   - The **growth of £1** line (portfolio vs benchmark) over the back-test.
   - An **underwater (drawdown) chart** under it, sharing the time axis.
   - A **monthly returns heatmap** (years × months).
   - **Rolling 12-month return and volatility** lines.
   - A **worst-drawdowns table**: depth, start, trough, recovery, months to recover.
6. **Risk detail.**
   - A **correlation heatmap** of holdings, ordered so that similar holdings cluster.
   - **Risk contribution vs weight** bars, showing which holdings drive the ups and downs.
7. **Income and fees.** Monthly **dividends, interest and fees** for the last 12 months: grouped bars, fees shown
   below zero.
8. **Holdings table.**
   - Columns: name, account, value, weight, 5-year return, volatility, Sharpe and history length.
   - Sortable, with short-history rows flagged.
9. **Data and caveats.** The `warnings`, price-file provenance, risk-free rate, lookback, and "analysis, not advice;
   no tax considered".

Charts must be readable in greyscale print: use patterns, dash styles, markers and direct labels, never colour
alone. Every chart has a text alternative (caption or matching table).

## 5. Wording and number rules

- Money is £ with thousands separators. Use whole pounds in tables; in large headline figures, rounding to £k is
  fine.
- Percentages to 1 dp for weights and 2 dp for returns and volatility; Sharpe to 2 dp; drawdowns negative with a
  true minus sign.
- **"Volatility"** means annualised standard deviation of monthly returns. **"Sharpe ratio"** means (annual return
  − risk-free rate) / volatility. Define both on the page.
- **Avoid:**
  - "guaranteed", "safe";
  - "you should", "we recommend";
  - "beat the market";
  - forecasts of future returns.
- Synthetic sample names are placeholders ("Example Platform B"). Real pages show real provider and fund names.

## 6. Edge cases

- A holding with less than 5 years of prices: shorter-history flag; frontier statistics use the overlap.
- No price file for a holding: it is valued but left out of the frontier, with a warning.
- Cash-only accounts; negative cash (pending settlement).
- Max-Sharpe mix equal to the current mix (no trades): show "no change".
- Very small trades below a threshold: hidden behind "show all".
- USD holdings: currency exposure explains part of their volatility.

## 7. Theme, print and accessibility

- Reuse the Paper theme's light, dark and print tokens. Dark mode follows the system; the "Theme" control is
  shared with the results page.
- **Save as PDF** works as on the results page: black and white, A4, with no card, chart or table row split, and
  headings kept with their content.
- Keyboard-usable controls, visible focus, semantic tables, and WCAG AA contrast in light, dark and print.

## 8. Privacy

Real data contains account owners, providers, holdings and values. Design only with the synthetic sample. The page
must never send data anywhere: no analytics, external fonts, scripts or images.
