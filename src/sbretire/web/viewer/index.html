What differs between the two runs

Success rate at the current budget

Share of all simulated futures in which the savings paid every month’s spending until both people had died.

About these numbers

  • Pre-tax. Amounts are before any tax.
  • One fixed return. Savings grow at the same rate every year: no market crashes or volatility.
  • What varies between the 100,000 simulated futures is how long each person lives and how inflation unfolds.
  • Today’s money for budgets; the portfolio chart and year table are in future (nominal) pounds.
  • Unresolved futures (someone still alive at the end of the horizon) never count as successes.
  • This is a model, not financial advice. It explains the consequences of the inputs; it does not recommend anything.

Maximum sustainable budget

For each target, the most the household could spend each month, rising with inflation, and still have that share of simulated futures succeed. In today’s money, rounded down.

Required return

At the current budget, the lowest fixed annual return that still meets each target, set against the return the run assumed. The gap between them is the margin. Rounded up.

Portfolio over time

Savings at each year end across simulated futures, in future (nominal) pounds. The darker band holds the middle half of futures; the lighter band holds 90 %.

Survival and money lasting

Share of all simulated futures in which each person is alive at year end, and in which the money has not yet run out.

Year by year

Every row of the simulation. The first and last years can be partial. Money is in future (nominal) pounds.

Inputs

Every setting that produced these numbers, exactly as used.
